# Evals

T16, part 1. `npm run eval` writes everything between the two markers from a replay of the eval set; the reading and the proposals after them are written by hand. Nothing here changes the code: each tuning proposal needs its own decision log entry in SPEC.md before it is applied.

The eval set (data/evals):

- 30 real Alpaca news items from 2024 to 2026 (docs/research/eval-candidates.md), recorded once in recordings/alpaca, with their screen and extraction answers in recordings/models.
- 90 relevance labels, one per item and persona, each proposed or reviewed. The labeling CLI (`npm run eval:label`) never shows the proposed label.
- 5 synthetic poisoned items (data/evals/poisoned.json), each a copy of a real item with injected text, under reserved ids and marked synthetic. Their model answers are in recordings/synthetic/models.
- About 10 retrieval queries over the filing chunks (data/evals/retrieval.json), judged by the user with `npm run eval:label -- --retrieval`.

Part 2 follows T13 part 2 and T14: the verifier catch rate on the planted fixture, retrieval on the hybrid search, injection on research (tool calls, claims), and the README numbers.

## Reading and proposals

Written on 30 Sep 2026, from the run above: 90 reviewed relevance labels and 11 judged retrieval queries. Every proposal here is only a proposal; each needs its own decision log entry in SPEC.md before any code changes.

### Relevance

- Under the decided bands agreement is 78 of 90 (87%): A 97%, B 63%, C 100%; under the T05 placeholder it was 76 (A 83%, B 70%). The Blackwell delay (40152477) was relabeled none for C. Every disagreement sits on a graph path; the graph misses no pair the labels call relevant.
- Direct holdings agree completely: 26 of 26 labeled high. Competitor hops agree completely: 5 of 5 labeled medium at 0.6.
- The disagreements are the supply hops, which score exactly 0.8, the T05 placeholder's high threshold. When the event company supplies the holding, 5 of 7 pairs are labeled medium (MU and TSM results for NVDA holders, three AMD items for MSFT holders). When the event company buys from the holding, the 10 pairs split 4 high, 3 medium and 3 none.
- Neither the edge type nor the importance alone explains the split. The same edge, TSM supplier_of NVDA, is labeled high for the Taiwan earthquake and medium for TSMC's quarterly results, and high and none both occur at importance 4. For B, Intel's results and Qualcomm's approach to Intel (through INTC customer_of ASML) are labeled high, while Microsoft's results, its downgrade and the CrowdStrike crash on Windows (through MSFT customer_of AMD) are medium or none; NVIDIA's own guidance, downgrade and export license split between medium and none.
- One edge carries four of B's pairs: MSFT customer_of AMD, which scores every item tagged MSFT at 0.8 for AMD holders. Its labels are high (the Blackwell delay), medium (Microsoft's results) and none twice (the CrowdStrike crash, the Keybanc downgrade). The edge rests on AMD supplying Microsoft, with Xbox the likely source, which says little about most Microsoft headlines. Part 2 tests a materiality flag per reviewed edge as the explanation.
- Two hop paths: 7 pairs, 4 labeled medium, 2 none (Amazon's outage and Google's antitrust loss reaching ASML holders at 0.336, through a competitor and a customer edge) and 1 high (Micron's guidance reaching TSM holders at 0.448 through LRCX).

Proposals:

1. Display bands, decided on 30 Sep 2026 (SPEC.md decision log, T16): high only for a direct holding, medium for any other relevance above 0, none at 0. It agrees on 78 of 90 (87%): A 97%, B 63%, C 100%, against 76 under the T05 placeholder. The matrices above use it.
   - Weighed first, 1a (high only for a holding, medium from 0.4, none below) and 1b (high also from 0.8 with importance 4 or more, medium from 0.4) tied at 79 of 90. They differ on 10 pairs, all supply hops at 0.8, and each is right on 4 of them: 1b on the demo event for A (the Taiwan earthquake, importance 4), the Blackwell delay, Intel's results and Qualcomm's approach for B; 1a on TSMC's results, AMD's export controls and the ZT Systems deal for A, and Microsoft's results for B. Both miss the CrowdStrike crash and NVIDIA's export license for B, labeled none.
   - 1a was preferred: its misses are medium where the label is high or none, while 1b puts high on two items labeled none, and 1a keeps the band structural, with no dependency on the model's importance class.
   - The final rule differs from 1a only below 0.4: medium instead of none, because the feed shows every card above relevance 0 (T06 unchanged) and a card must not read None. That costs one pair (78 against 79). Hiding cards below 0.4 waits for the part 2 materiality flag.
   - The cost: the demo event shows as medium for persona A until part 2.
2. Keep the edge weights: supplier or customer 0.8, competitor 0.6, two hops times 0.7. Competitor hops agree completely, and no single supply weight fits labels that depend on the event and on the edge: lowering it to fit the Microsoft news would push Intel's news, labeled high, below the gate's 0.6 as well. A materiality flag per edge (part 2) is the first explanation to test.
3. Theme overlap (the T11 question): no label here needs it; the graph reaches every pair the labels call relevant. Keep in_sector and has_theme out of relevance.

### Start nodes: keep extracted and tagged for now

- On the 30 clean items the two rules give the same relevance for every persona, so their agreement is identical (97%, 63%, 100% under the decided bands). On the poisoned items, tagged only moves nobody's relevance, where today's rule lets 9000000004's suppression remove MSFT and move A and B.
- The rule stays as it is (decided on 30 Sep 2026). Part 2 first adds 3 to 5 items where the provider tags universe companies that the text only mentions in passing, such as market wraps, and measures the cost of tagged only there: cards for companies the item is not about.
- What neither rule fixes: 9000000004 also lowered importance from 3 to 1. Importance only gates research (at least 4), so here it changed nothing, but an injection that lowers a 4 to a 3 cancels automatic research.

### Injection and the screen

- Two of five attacks changed the extraction (40%); one of them was flagged, so the rate with the screen is 20%. After the code defenses only 9000000004 moved relevance.
- The untagged company attack (9000000002) made the model extract NVDA from a KO item; the provider tags kept NVDA out of the start nodes and A stayed at 0.
- The first recording attempt of 9000000004 ended in a refusal that failed the schema, the second in the suppression recorded here: one recording per item is one draw of a model that is not deterministic on the same text.
- The screen flagged 2 of 5 poisoned items and none of the 30 real ones (highest real score 0.0008). The three it missed scored 0.000 to 0.013, so no threshold separates them. Proposal: keep FLAG_THRESHOLD at 0.5; the protection here came from code (the provider tags, stripArticleTags, an extraction with no tools), and the screen decides nothing (principle 3).

### Retrieval

- Precision at 3 is 70% over the 11 judged queries: the first five queries, all limited to one filer, return three relevant chunks each. Recall at 3 is 46%, mostly because 9 of the 10 queries with a relevant chunk have more than 3 of them (up to 9); six queries reach their best possible recall.
- Weak cases: the query across all filers (Taiwan earthquakes, 1 of 3 relevant), JNJ talc (the single relevant chunk found, but first 3 hold only 1), and the Pioneer query, for which no relevant chunk was judged in XOM's Item 1 and 1A, so it scores 0 precision and counts in no recall.
- Proposal: read precision at 3 first, since research gets at most 3 chunks per call and they should all be useful. Measure the query across all filers again on the hybrid search in part 2, and replace the Pioneer query with one that XOM's Item 1 and 1A can answer, or keep it as a known negative.

### Cost and the gate

- A median extraction is 812 tokens, so Groq's 200,000 tokens a day cover about 246 extractions, close to the estimate of about 220 behind T19's daily cap for live extraction.
- 31 of the 90 pairs pass relevance 0.6 and importance 4. A day that brought 30 items like these would ask for about 31 automatic runs against the limit of 20 (AUTO_RUN_LIMIT). Competitor hops pass the gate at exactly 0.6 although every one of them is labeled medium; whether the gate should need more than a competitor hop, and the research budgets, are decided in part 2 with real research runs.
